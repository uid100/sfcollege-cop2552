The Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) also called the System Development Life Cycle describes the engineering phases that all mature processes need to follow.

It has always amused me that it has not been standardized. To prove the point, a quick web search resulted in this:

Although there doesn't appear to be consensus, This fundamental concept, belongs in every engineering interview discussion, and the candidate should grasp the opportunity to discuss the finer points.

Although the names of the phases, boundaries, and endpoints may vary, there is general agreement of the activities. If you are headed to an interview, I'll leave it to you to read the different interpretations and decide whether there is agreement.

Generally speaking the activities can be summarized as:

  • Concept development and Requirements Definition
  • Solution Design
  • Implementation
  • Testing
  • Maintenance and Support

Whether a project is following a traditional waterfall project methodology with well-defined entry- and exit-criteria for clear phase boundaries, or taking a more agile approach, it is generally recognized that all of the phases will have some overlap. Requirements may need refinement in the Testing phase, supportability concerns should be identified during project inception, and test activity happens throughout.

Ultimately, most will agree that some amount of planning needs to take place before launching blindly into development, and a product should be fully tested after development claims "done."

 

When you approach a new software project, you should start with some amount of planning. What needs to be done? What are the requirements? What will need to be tested? Have you seen any sample code that could be modified as a prototype?

Direct actions that you can take now.

  • Read the description again
  • Make a list of the requirements
  • Look at the due date (again). Do you have schedule challenges?
  • Open the rubric. Do you need to add anything to your requirements list?

Track your progress. Half of the number of days remaining does not mean the project should be half-way to completion. But it shouldn't be at the starting gate either.
